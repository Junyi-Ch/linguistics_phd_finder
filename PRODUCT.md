# PRODUCT.md — Linguistics PhD Program Explorer

_Adapted from the Communication explorer's product notes. Assumptions are marked._

## What it is
A free, static web tool that helps prospective PhD applicants in Linguistics find US programs and faculty whose
research matches their own interests. It is the sister site of the Communication PhD Explorer and shares its code.
Linguistics applicants often belong as much to an adjacent unit (computational linguistics in CS, applied
linguistics, a Hispanic or East Asian linguistics track, speech and hearing science) as to a Linguistics department,
so those units are included and always labelled as such.

## Who uses it (assumption)
- Primary: applicants preparing a statement of purpose, often international (many Chinese-speaking), using a
  laptop in the evening with a Google Doc open next to the browser. They know their topic but not the US map of
  departments. Task frequency: a few intense sessions during application season (Sept–Dec), then occasional.
- Secondary: faculty advisors and master's program staff recommending programs; curious scholars.

## Jobs to be done
1. "Here is my research in my own words — who could advise me, and at which programs?"
2. "Which programs have at least 2–3 plausible advisors for me?"
3. "Browse all programs and all faculty by subfield / method / data / population / language."
5. "Who works on *my* language, whatever their department?"
4. "Keep a shortlist and export it to my application spreadsheet."

## Product truth and constraints
- All matching runs in the browser (transformers.js: arctic-embed-m + MiniLM re-ranker, ~130 MB one-time download); nothing is sent to a server.
- Data: every US university with a research PhD in Linguistics (~55, from the LSA directory and verified on official
  pages) plus adjacent units with a research PhD and language faculty; tenure-line faculty (assistant / associate /
  full) scraped from official directories; labels generated by an LLM from public profiles and marked as
  interpretive aids with sources. No rankings are shown.
- Static site (Astro) on GitHub Pages; must work on phones; light and dark themes; English UI with 中文 toggle.
- No accounts, no tracking beyond the owner's existing analytics choice (none by default).

## Brand commitments
- Honest about limits: every record shows its source and scrape date; labels are "interpretive, not exhaustive".
- Calm, precise, data-forward; no marketing hype, no ranking worship (rankings are shown as one signal with year).
- Owner: Junyi Chen (apc003@ucsd.edu). Built on the Communication PhD Explorer by Junyu Jiang.

## Success
An applicant pastes 150 words and within a minute has a shortlist of programs with named potential advisors and
reasons, and can export it.
